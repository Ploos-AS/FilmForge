# Dead Channel — Semifinal Stress Test

## Audience promise
There is one radio frequency every civilization eventually discovers — and the dead are still talking on it.

## Protagonist
Sera Vale, the sole technician at an aging deep-space listening station.

**Want:** finish her final rotation without another incident and return home.
**Wound:** she ignored the last personal message from her estranged brother before his death.

## Opening image
Sera eats alone beneath a wall of receivers. Every speaker is active except one physically disconnected unit. It whispers her name.

## First two minutes
A routine spectrum sweep crosses the prohibited frequency. We hear ordinary domestic chatter from a civilization catalogued as extinct 612 years ago. Sera kills the receiver.

The disconnected speaker answers something she mutters under her breath.

## Midpoint discovery
The broadcasts are not recordings. Sera can converse with one speaker, but its civilization believes *humanity* vanished centuries ago.

## Ending
Sera breaks protocol to warn the other voice about an event in its near future. Contact dies.

As rescue arrives, every receiver on the station lights simultaneously. Thousands of voices from different dead worlds say the same sentence in their own languages:

"We heard you."

## Hard rules
1. The channel is universally detectable but cannot be localized in normal space.
2. Passive reception is common; two-way contact is exceptionally rare and has consequences.
3. Broadcasts come from civilizations independently verified as extinct or nonexistent in the listener's present.

## Visual signature
Analog-like radio hardware embedded in believable future technology, lonely landscapes, spectral displays, darkness and ordinary voices against cosmic silence.

## Future films
- **Listeners:** a clandestine organization has catalogued successful replies for generations.
- **The Quiet World:** explorers reach a supposedly dead planet whose channel is still broadcasting.
- **Last Transmission:** humanity hears its own ordinary future conversations on the channel.

## Stress result
Exceptional first-film fit and ending. The long-term mythology must remain science-fictional and rule-driven rather than becoming supernatural.
