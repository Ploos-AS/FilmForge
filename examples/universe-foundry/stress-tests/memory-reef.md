# Memory Reef — Semifinal Stress Test

## Audience promise
The dead leave behind places made from memory — and sometimes those places remember people who were never there.

## Protagonist
Talin Rook, a licensed wreck salvager who avoids memory reefs after losing her mother in a passenger-ship disaster.

**Want:** complete one valuable salvage and clear her debts.
**Wound:** she never recovered her mother's body and refuses to use reef memories as substitutes for the dead.

## Opening image
A black wreck hangs in space covered in luminous branching crystal. Inside one crystal facet, a family breakfast silently repeats.

## First two minutes
Talin boards a newly discovered wreck before official reef specialists arrive. Touching nothing, she watches fragments of strangers' final hours flicker through the corridors.

Then a memory shows Talin herself walking aboard the ship.

The wreck is older than she is.

## Midpoint discovery
The reef does not merely preserve memories from the dead. Some fragments belong to people connected to the wreck through events that have not happened yet.

## Ending
Talin escapes with evidence but deliberately leaves the most valuable crystal untouched.

From outside, the reef blooms across the wreck. One new facet shows her mother, older than when she died, looking directly outward.

## Hard rules
1. Reefs form only around sites of mass death involving complex minds.
2. A memory fragment is experiential, incomplete and cannot be treated as objective recording.
3. Future or impossible memories are rare and indicate a deeper property of the reef, not routine prophecy.

## Visual signature
Dark mechanical wrecks invaded by luminous crystalline growth, intimate memories reflected in surfaces and abrupt contrasts between warmth and vacuum.

## Future films
- **The Collector:** illegal memory markets begin manufacturing identities from reef fragments.
- **Oldest Reef:** an expedition finds a reef predating known intelligent life.
- **The Person Who Remembers You:** someone assembles a complete personality from memories belonging to different dead people.

## Stress result
Outstanding emotional and visual identity. Needs disciplined rules so memory phenomena never become a convenient answer to any plot.
