# Pale Transit — Semifinal Stress Test

## Audience promise
The gates move you instantly — so why do strangers remember spending weeks together on the journey?

## Protagonist
Jon Arlen, a transit-systems investigator whose job is to explain away passenger anomalies.

**Want:** close an embarrassing incident before it threatens public trust in the gate network.
**Wound:** he has recurring memories of a childhood journey his family insists never occurred.

## Opening image
Two strangers emerge from adjacent gate booths, see each other across a crowded terminal, and simultaneously begin crying.

## First two minutes
Jon interviews them separately. Both know private facts about the other. Both remember a seventeen-day journey through a white transit vessel.

Both remember a third passenger named Nia.

No Nia entered either gate.

## Midpoint discovery
Security records contain single-frame images of the three passengers somewhere that is not the departure or arrival terminal. Jon recognizes the place from his childhood memories.

## Ending
Jon proves the two passengers could not have met in normal space and suppresses the report under orders.

On his next gate crossing, the normal instantaneous flash lasts several seconds.

A woman waits in the white corridor.

"You're late," says Nia.

## Hard rules
1. Public gate transit is observably instantaneous in normal spacetime.
2. Pale-transit memories are mutually consistent between affected travelers.
3. Physical objects normally cannot return from the hidden journey, making the first verified object a major future event.

## Visual signature
Busy practical terminals, brutal clean gate machinery, momentary white corridors that feel physical rather than dreamlike, and impossible travel artifacts.

## Future films
- **Lost Property:** the first physical object returns from pale transit.
- **Platform Zero:** investigators search for passengers who entered gates but never arrived.
- **The Long Crossing:** an entire ship experiences years inside what observers see as an instantaneous jump.

## Stress result
Excellent mystery structure and manageable first film. Requires a distinctive design language to avoid generic liminal-space aesthetics.
