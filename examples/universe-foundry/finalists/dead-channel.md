# Dead Channel — Finalist Development

## Core promise
Every civilization eventually discovers the same impossible radio frequency. It carries ordinary voices from worlds independently known to be dead.

## World bible
Humanity is an established interstellar civilization linked by slow physical travel and conventional high-bandwidth communications. The Dead Channel is the one phenomenon no accepted model explains.

Discovery of the frequency is old enough to have protocols, folklore and classified research, but successful two-way contact is extraordinarily rare. Publicly, transmissions are treated as anomalous archival leakage. Privately, authorities know some broadcasts react to listeners.

The universe remains materially grounded. The channel is not magic, an afterlife, or proof of souls. Its mechanism is unknown, but its behavior is measurable and constrained.

## Film #1 — 18 minute beat sheet
**0:00–1:30 — The impossible speaker.** Sera Vale works alone at listening station Kestrel. A disconnected receiver whispers her name.

**1:30–4:00 — The forbidden frequency.** A diagnostic sweep catches domestic conversation from a civilization extinct for 612 years. Sera shuts it down.

**4:00–6:30 — Reply.** The disconnected receiver answers a remark Sera makes aloud. She tests it with information not present in station systems.

**6:30–9:00 — The other listener.** A voice named Cael believes Sera is the anomaly. In his records, humanity vanished centuries ago.

**9:00–11:30 — Verification.** Sera confirms astronomical and historical details proving Cael's world cannot coexist with her present history.

**11:30–14:30 — The warning.** Cael casually mentions a near-future event matching the last known hours of his civilization. Sera can warn him, violating protocol.

**14:30–16:30 — Choice.** Sera transmits the warning. The channel destabilizes. Cael asks why she would risk herself for someone already dead.

**16:30–17:30 — Silence.** Contact disappears. Sera records an incident report, then deletes the safe version and submits the truth.

**17:30–18:00 — Aperture.** Every receiver activates. Thousands of voices in different languages say the equivalent of: "We heard you."

## Character arc
Sera begins believing unanswered personal messages are safer left unanswered. Contact with Cael forces her to choose response over regret. Her action does not resolve grief over her brother; it changes what she does with it.

## Reveal budget
Film #1 answers:
- the broadcasts can contain responsive intelligence;
- at least one speaker experiences humanity as extinct;
- contact can alter channel behavior.

Film #1 does NOT answer:
- what creates the channel;
- whether speakers occupy alternate histories, times or something else;
- why civilizations appear dead to one another;
- who or what coordinates the final chorus.

## Visual development
Grounded frontier technology; tactile controls; restrained displays; enormous darkness outside small human spaces. The channel should be visually represented through behavior of equipment and spectral patterns, not glowing supernatural energy.

Signature image: one person surrounded by hundreds of receivers lighting one by one.

## GenAI / continuity risk
Low-to-medium. One primary human character, one main physical location, secondary character mostly voice-only. Biggest risks are maintaining station geography, prop consistency and readable screens.

## Three-film test
1. **Dead Channel** — Sera makes first verified reciprocal contact.
2. **Listeners** — investigators discover generations of suppressed successful replies and conflicting models of the phenomenon.
3. **Last Transmission** — humanity begins receiving mundane broadcasts apparently originating from its own extinct future.

## Franchise health
Excellent. Each film can focus on a different listener/world while preserving an overarching mystery.
