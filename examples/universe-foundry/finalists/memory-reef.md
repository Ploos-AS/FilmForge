# Memory Reef — Finalist Development

## Core promise
Sites of mass death in space can grow luminous structures that replay fragments of human memory.

## World bible
Memory reefs are a known but poorly understood phenomenon around some major wrecks. Their crystalline growth incorporates ship material and expands slowly through interiors.

Reef fragments reproduce subjective experiences: sensations, emotions, images and partial thoughts. They are legally unreliable as evidence because memory is incomplete and perspective-bound.

Salvage law strictly regulates reefs. Families, historians, corporations and black markets all value fragments for different reasons.

Rare "impossible memories" depict people or events that should have no relationship to the wreck. Their existence is disputed.

## Film #1 — 18 minute beat sheet
**0:00–1:30 — Breakfast in the wreck.** Salvager Talin Rook approaches a dead liner overgrown with luminous crystal. In one facet she sees a silent family breakfast.

**1:30–4:00 — Illegal entry.** Talin boards before reef authorities arrive, needing one valuable salvage to clear her debts.

**4:00–6:30 — The dead remember.** Warm fragments of passengers' lives appear across cold corridors. Talin refuses to engage with them emotionally.

**6:30–8:00 — Talin aboard.** A crystal shows Talin walking through the same corridor. The wreck predates her birth.

**8:00–11:00 — Verification.** Ship records confirm the image cannot be a normal memory. Talin follows repeating fragments toward the wreck's core.

**11:00–13:30 — Her mother.** She sees her mother, who died on another ship, speaking to someone outside the memory's visible frame.

**13:30–16:00 — The valuable fragment.** Talin can cut out the crystal and solve her financial problems, but doing so will destroy the surrounding memory structure.

**16:00–17:15 — Choice.** She leaves it intact and escapes as unstable growth consumes the salvage route.

**17:15–18:00 — Aperture.** From outside, a new crystal blooms. Talin's mother appears decades older than when she died and looks directly outward.

## Character arc
Talin begins by treating memory as contamination and ends willing to confront it without claiming it is objective truth.

## Reveal budget
Film #1 answers:
- reefs reproduce subjective memory;
- the phenomenon can contain impossible memories;
- removing fragments damages contextual memory structures.

Film #1 does NOT answer:
- what the reef organism/material actually is;
- why impossible memories occur;
- whether Talin's mother survives anywhere;
- whether reefs predict, reconstruct or connect.

## Visual development
Mechanical darkness invaded by branching luminous crystal. Memory scenes should appear embedded/reflected in material rather than as generic holograms. Warm human memories contrast with cold wreck interiors.

Signature image: a dead spacecraft transformed into a glowing reef, with tiny domestic scenes visible inside its crystalline branches.

## GenAI / continuity risk
Medium-to-high. The reef itself needs a stable visual grammar, and embedded memory scenes create compositing challenges. Character consistency across age/state variants is significant.

## Three-film test
1. **Memory Reef** — Talin discovers impossible memories.
2. **The Collector** — a black market assembles identities from harvested memory fragments.
3. **Oldest Reef** — an expedition finds a reef containing memories older than known intelligent life.

## Franchise health
Excellent. Strong emotional anthology potential and iconic imagery, with higher production complexity than the other finalists.
