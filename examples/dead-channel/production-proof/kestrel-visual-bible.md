# Kestrel Listening Station — Visual Bible

## Design thesis
Kestrel is a working communications facility, not a glamorous spacecraft. It should feel maintained, repaired and inhabited by one person.

## Core spaces
1. Listening room — hero location; receiver wall, central console, exterior viewport.
2. Equipment corridor — narrow service access, cable trunks and removable panels.
3. Galley/sleep nook — small human counterpoint to technical spaces.
4. Airlock / docking vestibule — only needed for arrival/rescue framing.
5. Exterior — compact station attached to an oversized antenna structure.

## Visual rules
- practical industrial construction;
- restrained neutral materials;
- no holographic interfaces;
- displays are readable, purposeful and not decorative;
- receiver wall contains physically distinct modules accumulated across station upgrades;
- lighting is motivated by fixtures, displays and emergency states.

## Geography
The listening room must be treated as a fixed set. Establish a canonical floor plan and four locked camera-reference angles before production.

The receiver wall is behind Sera's primary chair. Viewport is camera-left from the chair. Main entry is rear-right. The forbidden-channel receiver occupies a visually memorable but not conspicuous position.

## Signature states
Normal: quiet pools of work light.
Channel contact: equipment behavior changes, not room color.
Final chorus: receiver indicators activate sequentially across the wall; illumination emerges from believable hardware.

## Generation risk
Medium. Environment consistency across many shots requires fixed reference frames, but one dominant room dramatically reduces location drift.
