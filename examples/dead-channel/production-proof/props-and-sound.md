# Dead Channel — Props and Sound Identity

## Hero props

### Forbidden-channel receiver
Older physical receiver retained because its analog front end behaves differently from modern station equipment. Heavy enclosure, tactile tuning controls, small spectral display, physical speaker grille.

It must remain visually identical throughout the film.

### Main console
Modern but utilitarian communications workstation. Physical controls coexist with flat displays. Important UI states should be designed as assets and composited when possible rather than regenerated inside video.

### Personal message token
A small UI indicator representing Sera's unanswered message archive. It supports her character arc without becoming a plot device.

## Sound identity
Sound is a primary storytelling layer.

### Station
Low ventilation, relay clicks, occasional structure movement, distant equipment fans. Avoid constant cinematic spaceship rumble.

### Dead Channel
Never use horror whispers as the default. Voices should sound ordinary and materially transmitted. The unsettling quality comes from context, timing and impossible responsiveness.

A subtle recurring channel artifact may identify contact, but it should be reproducible and restrained.

### Silence
Use genuine reductions in station ambience around key listening moments.

### Final chorus
Begin with individual physical receivers waking at different positions in the stereo field. Voices accumulate without becoming a monster effect. Different languages and ages remain recognizably human/intelligent.

The final line-equivalent should feel coordinated without sounding perfectly synchronized.
